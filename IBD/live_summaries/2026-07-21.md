## 1. Market Pulse

**Tuesday, July 21 — Heavy distribution, market character change.** The market was in the midst of a correction that began the prior week. NASDAQ struggling below its 50-day moving average after failing to reclaim it several times. S&P 500 also breaking below key levels. The Dow Jones was the relative outperformer — holding better than the tech-heavy indexes.

**Guest: David Ryan** — three-time US Investing Champion, former protégé of William O'Neil. Ryan brought his characteristic hard-nosed realism: "Don't buy the dip. This is like 2000."

**Panel:** Ed Carson, Ken Shreve, Justin Nielsen, and David Ryan (guest). David Saito-Chung and Chris Gessel supporting.

**Core message from David Ryan:**
- Growth stocks and AI names severely broken — many down 40-50% from highs
- "When the leaders get hit this hard, you don't buy the dip — you wait for them to build bases and prove themselves again"
- Ryan was actively shorting select names, especially those that rallied for 2 days to moving average resistance
- Two-tier market: defensives, financials, and value names at highs while growth/AI crashed
- Compared to 2000 when tech rolled over but the broader market didn't collapse until later

**Panel sentiment:** All panelists agreed — don't fight the trend. Cash is king. Buying dips in this environment is dangerous. Wait for follow-through day.

## 6. Trading Education — David Ryan Masterclass

**The "Don't Buy the Dip" Rule:** "When the market changes character and the leaders are getting destroyed, buying dips is how you go broke. Wait for them to build bases. It takes weeks and months, not days."

**The 50-Day Short Strategy:** "When a former leader rallies for 2 days up to its declining 50-day moving average and fails, that's a short setup. I've been doing this with NBIS and others."

**The 2000 Comparison:** "This reminds me of 2000 in some ways — not that we'll have a crash like that, but the two-tier nature. Tech rolling over while value and defensives go up. The money doesn't leave the market — it rotates. But eventually, if tech keeps getting hit hard enough, it drags everything down. That's what happened in late 2000."

**Good News Ignored = Bearish:** "When INTC reports +500% EPS growth and the stock goes nowhere, that's a major red flag. The market is telling you it doesn't care about good news anymore."

**Cash is a Position:** "People think they always have to be doing something. They don't. Cash is the best position right now in growth stocks. You'll know when it's time to get back in — the market will tell you with a follow-through day."
