## 1. Market Pulse

**Thursday, July 23 — Correction deepens.** Pre-market: NASDAQ (QQQ) down 1.6%, S&P 500 (SPY) down 1.1%, Dow (DIA) down 1.2%, Russell 2000 (IWM) down 1.2%. S&P set to go through the 50-day line; Dow approaching its 50-day; IWM opening at or below the 50-day. NASDAQ risk of heading toward June lows.

**The big catalyst:** 10-year Treasury yield (TNX) rising to 4.71% — a new **18-month high**. Justin Nielsen noted that the 30-year (TYX) is at **18-year highs** going back to 2009. Bond yields breaking out with a handle on TNX. And oil: US crude up ~5% to **$91/barrel**, Brent crude up 6% topping **$100 intraday**. Houthi rebels in Yemen (backed by Iran) claimed an attack on two Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea, threatening the Saudi pipeline that bypasses the Persian Gulf. Trump said to hold Iran responsible. Fed rate hike probability for September surged to **81%** — a hike this month was 36% and rising.

**Panel:** Ken Shreve (host), Arnie Gutierrez, Justin Nielsen (Head of Market Research), Ed Carson (News Editor). David Saito-Chung and Chris Gessel in the background answering Q&A.

**Market School System (Webster via Nielsen):** Buy switch **off** on NASDAQ — needs a follow-through day or to clear the highs from about a month ago. Power trend still **on** for S&P 500 (VOO) and RSP (equal-weight S&P 500) — indicating a **two-tier market** reminiscent of post-March 2000 where tech was rolling over but money rotated into Dow/S&P names that didn't roll until late in the year.

**Panel Sentiment:** Justin Nielsen: "Not really much reason to stick your neck out... things work for a couple days and then they turn over. David Ryan lets something rally for 2 days and then starts shorting." Ed Carson: "Cash is generally king. Probing buys are fine." Ken Shreve: "Super challenging out there." All panel members at very low exposure.

## 6. Trading Education

**Justin Nielsen — Cash is a Position:** "When it gets challenging, I don't usually like to play as much... I'll still put on a trade here and there more as a probing position. Right now I'm not getting traction. I've raised a lot of cash personally."

**Ed Carson — Why Uptrends Matter:** "The reason we like uptrends is because the market trends up. If you buy on strength in a choppy market, really good chance you'll see it roll over. That's what's happened."

**Arnie Gutierrez — Defensive Strategy:** "You have to balance everything out. Until this market proves it wants to go up, I'm not going to be as aggressive... Just be careful. If you don't feel comfortable, don't force yourself to trade. Protect your mental capital."

**Mike Webster — NASDAQ Chart Analysis:** Explained the "market school" system — buy switch off means need a follow-through day OR to clear highs from about a month ago. Against the selloff, that downside reversal point becomes the fail-safe. The system was built to handle "what if this happens" scenarios — Long-Term Capital Management-type thinking.

**Mike Webster — Two-Tier Market Lesson:** "Post-March 2000, the NASDAQ was rolling over. They were selling all the tech stocks, but money flows to a different place — into the Dow and S&P stocks. That didn't roll until late that year. You could be seeing a mini version of this."

**Mike Webster — Portfolio Management:** On SEZL: "Sold it just because I was getting killed with so many things... because of portfolio management, not because it's doing anything wrong." On NTAP: "Imagine this bar at the high of the day. If it's at the low, what would it look like at the high? That game tells you how to treat it."

**Chris Gessel — Hedging Strategy:** Uses MUZ (2x inverse Micron ETF) to hedge memory positions. "Now it makes it a whole lot easier to hedge Micron." On DELL: "Draw that trend line... if it gets tight with the 21-day and breaks through, that could be a good entry."

**Scott St. Clair — Psychological Honesty:** "In hindsight, I've had a lousy year when I look at what could have been... the SANDisks, the MUs. But if you told me on January 1 I'd be up X amount in July, I'd say OK, I'll take that. Very difficult psychologically... Markets have been a lot more difficult than it seems."
