### 6. Trading Education & Process (Psychology & Strategy)
- **The "Do Less, Not Nothing" Approach:**
    Justin Nielsen explained that when he's out of sync, his rule is not to stop completely (which he knows he can't stick to), but to "do less." This means smaller position sizes and only putting on probing positions. This allows him to stay engaged, get market feedback, and gradually rebuild confidence without taking large drawdowns.

- **The 1998 LTCM Playbook (Why You Need to Stay in the Game):**
    Scott St. Clair drew a powerful parallel between the recent hedge fund blow-up and the 1998 Long-Term Capital Management crisis. In both cases, massive, leveraged players in the hottest trade (then internet, now AI) were forced to liquidate, creating a violent but ultimate cleansing washout. The takeaway: these events create opportunities, but you can't participate if you've been wiped out yourself. *"When Genius Failed"* is the recommended book.

- **Fighting the "Fight or Flight" with Mechanical Stops:**
    Scott described his struggle with freezing when a position goes against him. His solution is to use Good-Till-Canceled (GTC) stop orders placed with his broker. He puts these on immediately. This removes the psychological "fight or flight" paralysis in the moment, forcing him to adhere to his risk rules on volatile names like NBIS.

- **Position Sizing as the Rosetta Stone for Volatility:**
    Scott explained that because stocks like NBIS have 12.5% intraday swings, he cannot use a normal stop or position size. He drastically reduces his position size so that a 12.5% stop equals the same dollar loss as a 5-8% stop on a normal position. This is the only way to "play in that sandbox."

- **The "Webby Bottom" (Flexibility in Patterns):**
    Mike Webster called Amazon's pattern a "double bottom in spirit," acknowledging it didn't undercut the first low. He quoted Bill O'Neill's flexibility: "It was so strong that it couldn't undercut." The lesson is to recognize the intention of the pattern (a shakeout) rather than being rigidly dogmatic about a perfect textbook undercut.

- **The Importance of the Equal-Weighted Index (RSP):**
    Webby stressed that the RSP is how he looks at the "true health of the market." While the NASDAQ has been a mess, the RSP's steady, sustainable uptrend is a much more reliable indicator. When the RSP's regression line gives a signal, it's safer to press the gas on broad-based instruments than to pick individual broken stocks.
