### 6. Trading Education & Process
- **The "Squat" and Holding Through Intraday Shakeouts:**
    Dave walked through a powerful lesson using **NTAP**. The stock broke out at $192.83, but by the next morning, it had traded as low as $180.10, a 6.6% intraday decline. Many would have stopped out. However, the stock quickly reversed and closed near highs, never touching even the 10-day simple moving average. The lesson: when the stock is a true leader and the market is constructive, give it room to the classic 7-8% sell rule and look at the daily chart, not just the first few minutes. The low of the first 5-minute bar can be a helpful gauge, but the broader trend matters more.

- **Don't Chase Extended Breakouts:**
    Ed repeatedly warned against chasing stocks that are 10-15% above the 50-day line with high ATRs, especially in the AI space. For example, CRDO looked great but was already 15%+ above the 50-day, making risk management difficult. Waiting for a handle or a pullback is the disciplined approach. "The big mistake here would be to chase."

- **Resistance at the 50-Day Line:**
    Many AI/semi stocks (MU, BE, AVGO, TSM, CRDO) are right at their 50-day lines. Justin and Ed both stated they will not buy a stock at that level unless it decisively clears the line and ideally builds a handle. The 50-day is a battleground; buying just below it is a low-reward, high-risk proposition.

- **Earnings Season: Sell the News / Buy the Rumor:**
    The day's action in Cisco, Coherent, and Cerebras highlighted the danger of holding into earnings after a strong run-up. Dave shared his own experience with **LQDA**: he exited before earnings, locking in a double-digit gain because the stock had run up and he didn't know the company well enough to hold through the binary event. "Buy the rumor, sell the news" is a recurring theme. Low-volume streaks of up days before earnings can also be a warning sign of a potential pullback.

- **Using the Index for Exposure While Stock-Specific Setups Develop:**
    Justin and Dave both emphasized using broad-based, leveraged ETFs (TQQQ, UPRO, MDY) to quickly get market exposure when the indexes are breaking out. This is especially useful when individual stocks are either extended or at key resistance. Once clear stock setups appear, you can rotate from ETFs into those names.

- **The "Weak Open, Strong Close" Dynamic:**
    The NTAP example also highlighted the concept of a weak open followed by a strong close, which is a sign of institutional accumulation. Justin and Dave both mentioned that this is the type of action you want to see in a bull market, and it's a reason not to panic on early morning weakness.
