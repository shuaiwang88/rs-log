### 1. Market Pulse
The market is in a **constructive digestion phase**, with the major indexes pulling back modestly after a powerful prior week. The Nasdaq composite fell 0.6%, while the S&P 500 and Dow slipped 0.3%, but these declines are seen as a normal, orderly shakeout after the recent run. Breadth was positive, with the equal-weight S&P 500 (RSP) and QQQE holding firm, even as mega-caps like Google and Amazon dragged the cap-weighted indexes lower. Ed Carson noted that the market is holding up well despite a slight rebound in oil prices, which is encouraging. Volatility is contracting near highs—a state Mike Webster has called "equilibrium"—which is constructive and sets the stage for clearer entry points when the market resolves its current tight range.

- **Overall Sentiment:** Constructive, not alarming. "Right now this is in some ways you could say it's a shakeout" (Ed). The key level to watch is the low of Tuesday's session; an undercut of that level would be more worrisome. Otherwise, the pause is healthy, allowing stocks to form handles and bases.

### 6. Trading Education & Process
- **The "Equilibrium" Pause:**
    Ed highlighted that after a powerful move, a tight, sideways consolidation (like the one in IWM and many individual stocks) is constructive. It reduces volatility and creates clearer buy points. "When it's tight like this, if it does move up or down, it becomes easier... it seems like a clear entry in a way that wider, looser markets" don't offer.

- **The 50-Day Line Test:**
    Using TSM and NVT as examples, Ed explained the critical juncture many AI stocks face. A decisive move above the 50-day line could be an entry signal, but false breakouts are common. The correct approach is to wait for confirmation, start with a smaller position, and not to bet the portfolio on a single sector even if multiple stocks trigger.

- **Focus on Quality, Not Just Price:**
    When discussing the after-hours pop in CoreWeave, Ali reminded viewers that the IBD style prioritizes quality stocks making powerful moves in a strong market. A laggard that pops on earnings can get back on the radar, but it's better to wait for it to prove itself with a proper base or handle rather than chasing the immediate gap.

- **Contextualizing a Pullback:**
    A 0.3-0.6% decline in the indexes after a multi-day, high-percentage rally is "normal digestion." It's crucial not to panic at minor red days; instead, use them to identify which stocks are holding their gains and showing relative strength, as those are the candidates for the next leg higher.
