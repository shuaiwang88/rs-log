### 6. Trading Education & Process
- **Don't Cheat at Resistance; Wait for Confirmation:**
    Ed delivered a key risk-management rule for the current environment: "Don't cheat just below a buy point or key levels." The semiconductor index (SMH) and many AI stocks are at their 50-day lines. The potential for them to be shorted right at this level is just as high as the potential for a breakout. The correct approach is to wait for a decisive, convincing move *above* the resistance rather than trying to buy in anticipation of it.

- **The "Sprint Before the Race" Exhaustion:**
    Using Coherent as an example, Ed explained that a stock that runs up 42% into earnings is like a "sprinter who's late for a race and has to run to the starting gate." It expends so much energy getting there that it's vulnerable to a sharp reversal on any news or profit-taking. This is a classic reason to avoid chasing a stock right before earnings, even if the technical setup looks superficially good. The risk/reward is poor.

- **The Value of a Constructive Pullback (Amazon's High Handle):**
    In contrast to Coherent, Amazon's post-earnings surge was followed by a contained, orderly high handle. This allows the stock to digest gains and the moving averages to catch up without violating any key support levels. A breakout above the high of this handle is a much lower-risk entry than buying a runaway gap-up.

- **Match Your Strategy to Headline Risk:**
    When trading geopolitically sensitive sectors like airlines (JETS) or energy (XLE), Ed advises using early entries and managing the trade like a swing trade. Because the next headline can instantly reverse the stock's direction, holding for a long-term, buy-and-hold move is exceptionally difficult without a massive profit cushion.
