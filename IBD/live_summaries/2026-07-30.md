## 1. Market Pulse

**Thursday, July 30 — Day 1 of a rally attempt.** A massive short-covering rally after a brutal sell-off triggered by the Fed meeting and Kevin Warsh commentary. Headline indexes surged: Nasdaq up nearly 3%, S&P 500 and QQQ posting strong gains, but the equal-weight RSP was DOWN ~1% at its lows — revealing a deeply lopsided, rotational bounce driven by mega-cap short-covering. Panel characterized it as a classic "bear market rally" or "401k bounce."

**The market-moving story of the day:** Leopold Aschenbrenner's hedge fund, **Situational Awareness LP**, was forced into complete liquidation after heavy AI-market losses. The fund was levered 4x. Prime brokers liquidated the entire public stock portfolio (and private stakes like Anthropic) to a single buyer to meet margin calls. Affected names included Nebius (NBIS), CoreWeave (CRWV), Micron (MU), SanDisk (SNDK), Bloom Energy (BE), AMD, Oracle, Bitcoin mining/AI transition equities (Riot, CleanSpark, Core Scientific), and short options against NVDA and SMH. This explained the cascading, "nonsensical" selling in AI stocks. Webster: "This is a really scary market, and I would not get excited about a big gap up today."

**Panel:** Mike Webster, Arnie Gutierrez, Ed Carson, David "Hatman" Saito Chung. Webster: "Stay in first or second gear. Use the emergency brake until a true follow-through day or sustained close above the 21-day." Arnie: "One day doesn't make a trend. Charts remain sprained or broken — they require proof before commitment." Hatman Dave: "We're in a correction, but it's a very lopsided correction. Some areas are actually pulling back just fine."

**Key technicals:** 10-year Treasury at 4.66%. Equal-weighted indexes (RSP, QQQE) holding above 21-day — much better than cap-weighted tech. Webster: "I don't pay attention to the bond market. Bill didn't use it, and I don't use it. Everyone else is using it, and that's factored into the price."

## 6. Trading Education & Guest Advice

### Mike Webster — 1999 Parallels & Correction Strategy

**1999 Bar-by-Bar Study:** Webster compared current action extensively to 1999 — a year of extreme rotations, violent sector moves, and a market where massive gains came only after months of chop. "You need to study 1999 bar-by-bar to understand choppy corrective behavior before major trends resume." In 1999, the first three quarters were volatile and trendless; the explosive fourth-quarter run rewarded those who stayed disciplined.

**A-Plus Setups Only:** "This is not a market for B-grade setups. Trade only A-plus setups. Lean on the 21-day moving average. Avoid heavy exposure during rotational churn."

**Gap-Up Skepticism:** "This is a really scary market. I would not get excited about a big gap up today. You're looking for a follow-through day, or you're looking to get above the 21-day. Stay in first or second gear — use the emergency brake."

**Hedge Fund Liquidation Playbook:** Track the list of stocks liquidated by Aschenbrenner's fund. "This is a valuable playbook for identifying beaten-down stocks that eventually bounce back once the forced selling clears." But wait for proper setups — don't buy just because they're down.

### Arnie Gutierrez — FOMO Management & Chart Integrity

**One Day ≠ Trend:** "One day doesn't make a trend. You want to make sure stocks are trading above both key moving averages. Many charts remain sprained or broken — they require proof before commitment."

**FOMO Warning:** Bear market bounces and short-covering rallies are seductive. "Don't let FOMO override what the chart is actually telling you. A stock bouncing 10% from deeply broken levels is still a broken chart until it proves otherwise."

**DDOG Setup:** Cup-with-handle, RSI blue dot, relative strength holding up. "This is the type of A-plus setup you wait for." But earnings in 7 days requires position sizing accordingly.

### Ed Carson — Exhaustive Earnings Review

Ed delivered a comprehensive pre-market earnings rundown covering dozens of companies across sectors: FTNT, TENB, QCOM, LRCX, ARM, SIMO, YUM, CMG, SBUX, CVNA, MA, BMY, VLO, PBF, SHEL, CRS, HOOD, WWD, GVA, PWR, JMKE. The key takeaway: mixed reactions — some beats rewarded (FTNT, SBUX), some beats sold (TENB, QCOM). "Beat-and-raise" is no longer an automatic win.

### Hatman Dave — Lopsided Corrections & Options Strategy

**Two-Tier Correction:** "We're in a correction, but it's a very lopsided correction. Some areas are actually pulling back just fine." Equal-weight indexes (QQQE, RSP) tell a different story from cap-weighted QQQ/SPY. Focus on where the rotational merchandise exists.

**Covered Calls in Thin Stocks:** Discussed lessons from his CRS experience. Covered calls in thinly traded, volatile names (CRS, BE) carry liquidity risk. "When a stock is doing a 40-50% correction like Bloom Energy, the option mechanics get tricky."
