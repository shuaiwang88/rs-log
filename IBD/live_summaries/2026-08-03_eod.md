### 6. Trading Education & Process
- **The "Probing Position" Approach in a Transitional Market:**
    Justin explained that in a choppy market where you've been out of sync, it's hard to go completely from cash to heavily invested. His mantra is "do less, not nothing." He puts on small probing positions. If they work and show traction, he adds. If they don't, he's out quickly with minimal damage. This allows him to stay engaged with the market and catch a new uptrend without taking on excessive risk.

- **News vs. Reaction to News:**
    A key lesson from the last two Mondays: identical positive geopolitical news produced completely opposite market reactions. Last Monday, a gap-up faded to new lows; today, gains built steadily and closed strong. The conclusion: "The news isn't as important as the reaction to the news." This is a foundational IBD principle for reading the market's true character.

- **Using Early Entries at Key Support:**
    For PSMT, Justin pointed out that an aggressive early entry at the 50-day bounce was viable because the risk was easily defined (a close below the 50-day). The breakout above $200 provides a second, more conservative entry. This layered approach allows traders of different risk tolerances to participate.

- **Selecting the Right ETF Based on Internal Strength:**
    When choosing between two similar sector ETFs (CIBR vs. HACK), Justin doesn't just look at the ETF's chart. He examines the charts of the top holdings. Because CRWD, FTNT, and PANW (the top holdings in CIBR) have stronger charts than AVGO and CSCO (the top holdings in HACK), CIBR is the preferred vehicle. This is a sophisticated application of relative strength analysis.

- **How the Leaders Hold Up Tells You Everything:**
    Justin studied VIK through the oil spike and noted that "leaders... resist going down even when the market indexes are putting pressure on them." When a stock refuses to break down on seemingly bad news, it's a powerful sign of institutional support and a leading candidate for the next leg higher.
