### 6. Trading Education & Process (Psychology & Strategy)
- **The Incremental Position Build:**
    David Ryan gave a detailed, real-time walkthrough of how he built a full position in CrowdStrike. Day 1: bought a tiny amount when it reclaimed a prior base. Day 2: added as it held. Day 3: added more as it tightened. Day 4: really increased the position on the breakout. Day 5: finished buying. This is the opposite of going "all in" at once; it uses market feedback to build confidence and size. He also showed ATEN as a contrast—it failed at the 50-day, so he never bought a share.

- **Sell on Price, Not Just Fundamentals:**
    David Ryan used the historical example of Zoom Video (ZM) in 2021. Even as the stock dropped 50% from its highs, earnings were still coming in at 500-1000% growth. The lesson: "I buy on price and earnings, but I sell mostly on price action." Just because earnings are strong doesn't mean the stock can't be a disaster.

- **The 50-Day Line as "No-Man's Land":**
    For former leaders that have broken down (MU, CAT, STX), Ryan emphasizes that they are in "no-man's land." He will not buy them until they can prove themselves by getting above and staying above the declining 50-day moving average. Until then, they are either watches or potential shorts on the tag of the 50-day.

- **Hedging Through Earnings (The Half-Hedge):**
    Chris Gessel's consistent strategy: When holding a core position into earnings, he puts on a half-hedge using puts. This reduces the "gap down" risk by 50% without selling the stock. This is a discipline that lets him hold through potential volatility and react to the news.

- **The Power of Earnings Lines for Long-Term Context:**
    Hatman used the monthly chart of Datadog to illustrate that even with periods of long underperformance, a stock with a strong earnings line can deliver a 35%+ compound annual growth rate. While the next breakout might yield a smaller percentage gain than prior ones, they are still very tradable, high-quality moves. This is the definition of "buying the right stock at the right time, over and over."
